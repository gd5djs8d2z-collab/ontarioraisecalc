About This Calculator

A raise calculator built around marginal tax rates — not gross salary estimates.

Why a Raise Calculator Is Different

Most salary calculators show you an estimated take-home pay for a given gross income. This tool does something different: it focuses on the portion of income that has changed — the raise itself — and shows you exactly how much of that increase you actually keep after Ontario and federal tax, CPP contributions, and EI premiums are applied to your new earnings.

That distinction matters. The tax rate on your raise is not the same as your overall effective rate. Because Canada uses a progressive tax system, each dollar of a raise is taxed at your marginal rate — the rate that applies to the top slice of your income. For an Ontario employee earning $75,000 and receiving a $10,000 raise, the relevant rates are 20.5% federal and 9.15% provincial, not the blended effective rate across their full salary. This calculator models that correctly.

The CPP and EI Ceiling Effect

Two of the deductions Canadians pay — CPP contributions and EI premiums — have annual maximums. Once your earnings cross those thresholds, the deductions stop for the rest of the year. In 2026, the CPP1 ceiling is $74,600 (YMPE), with a CPP2 tier applying on earnings up to $85,000 (YAMPE). The EI maximum insurable earnings are $68,900.

A raise that pushes your gross income across one of these thresholds has a different net impact than a raise that falls entirely below them. If you were previously earning $65,000 and receive a $20,000 raise, part of that raise interacts with the CPP1 ceiling, and the CPP2 rate applies to earnings between $74,600 and $85,000. This calculator accounts for those crossover effects when comparing your current and new salary — which a simple take-home pay estimator does not.

The 2026 Federal Rate Change

Starting in 2026, the bottom federal income tax bracket rate dropped from 14.5% to 14%. For employees earning below the first bracket threshold of $57,375, this means every dollar of a raise in that range is taxed at the new lower rate. The calculator uses the 2026 rates published by the Canada Revenue Agency — including this reduction — rather than carrying forward the 2024 or 2025 structure.

How the Calculation Works

You enter your current gross annual salary and your new gross annual salary. The calculator applies the full 2026 federal and Ontario tax bracket schedule, CPP contribution rules (including CPP2 where applicable), and EI premium rates to both figures. It then computes the difference in net pay — annual, monthly, and per pay period — and shows what each individual deduction contributes to that difference.

Every rate, threshold, and formula is stored in a single configuration file and referenced consistently throughout the tool. There are no hardcoded values in the calculation logic.

What the Results Tell You

The output shows your raise split into what you keep and what goes to each deduction category. It also shows your net pay increase as a percentage of your gross raise — your "raise retention rate." For most Ontario employees in mid-income ranges, this figure falls between 58% and 68%, depending on where the raise lands relative to bracket thresholds and CPP/EI ceilings.

Results are estimates based on standard employment assumptions and the basic personal amounts for 2026. They do not account for RRSP contributions, union dues, employer-paid benefits, or TD1 claims beyond the basic amount. Actual payroll outcomes will vary based on your specific employment situation.
